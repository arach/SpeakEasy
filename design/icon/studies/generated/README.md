# Generated concept exploration

These four boards were created with the harness's built-in image-generation
tool as ideation material. They are retained for review and provenance only.
No raster on these boards is read, traced, or embedded by the shipping icon
generator.

## Files

- `01-switchboard-routing.png` — voice-to-many routing, patch bays, plugs
- `02-speakeasy-deco.png` — Deco doors, apertures, keyhole-mouth, gramophone
- `03-breath-aperture.png` — breath, lips, hush, speaking thresholds
- `04-rotary-wildcards.png` — rotary selectors, safe/phone dials, hybrids
- `contact-sheet.png` — all four boards

## Prompt set

All four prompts used the `logo-brand` use case and requested a square 3-by-2
board of six original, vector-friendly concepts with large simple masses,
strong negative space, two to four flat colours, and potential 40 px legibility.
They prohibited text, labels, watermarks, device frames, third-party marks,
generic microphones, and generic five-bar waveforms.

The subject prompts were:

1. **Switchboard routing:** "Explore the switchboard / patch-bay idea of routing
   one human voice to many agent lines," including plugs, sockets, routing paths,
   one-to-nine fan-out, held talk controls, and compact switchboard topology.
2. **Speakeasy Deco:** "Explore prohibition-era speakeasy imagery," including
   art-deco door geometry, a sliding peephole or speaking aperture, a keyhole
   that is also a mouth, a compact gramophone horn, a coded knock, and restrained
   neon geometry.
3. **Breath and aperture:** "Explore breath, a speaking aperture, and quiet/hush,"
   including an opening emitting breath, lips in negative space, opposing human
   and agent apertures, an abstract hush gesture, a threshold, and a speaking
   iris.
4. **Rotary and wildcards:** "Explore rotary controls and unexpected hybrid
   symbols," including a nine-position selector, rotary telephone and safe-dial
   abstractions, voice routed around an indexed ring, privacy, and dispatch.

The strongest idea was the keyhole whose only aperture is a mouth. The final
CoreGraphics version simplifies it to one continuous outlined keyhole, a lip
path, and one almond aperture; it is an independent geometric reconstruction,
not a raster copy.
