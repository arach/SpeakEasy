WHAT A LANE HOLDS

Each key holds
whatever you put on it.

You pick what goes on each key. Today that's one exact Codex task — the key shows the project, the git branch, the last exchange, and whether that task is live, idle, or queued. Routing is by task ID, so nothing depends on which window is focused or what the most recent thread is called.

  • 01You assign it, and it stays assigned. A key keeps its task across reloads until you move it.
  • 02The task ID is the address. Each send revalidates the binding with Codex Desktop first.
  • 03It fails closed. If the binding can't be verified, the send stops — it won't pick a different task.
  • 04State is on the key. Live, idle, or queued, readable without opening anything.

MICRO DECK · 4×4 KEY BANK

More than nine tasks?
Use the 4×4.

The Micro Deck is a 4×4 key bank with the same binding model: one exact task per key, same fail-closed check. The bottom row is transport, which leaves twelve lanes.

THE BIG BAR

Your Mac does
the transcribing.

Hold the bar and talk. The iPad records the audio and hands it to your Mac over the local network, where SpeakEasy's Parakeet model produces the transcript. Nothing goes to a speech server. Let go and the transcript is sent once, to the task bound to that lane.

SAME CONVERSATION

Your dictation and Codex's reply stay in the same task. SpeakEasy handles voice and playback; it doesn't run a conversation of its own.

ROUTED BY TASK ID

Sends are addressed by exact task ID, not by title, recency, folder, or window focus. If the binding can't be verified, the send stops.

DURABLE QUEUE

The queue is on disk, so it survives a relaunch. What you said arrives once, in order, even if the iPad slept in between.

PLAYBACK

Answers come back
as audio you can scrub.

Scrub the reply, change the speed, replay the last exchange with RPLY, or turn on AUTO to have completions narrated as tasks finish. The full text is still in Codex; this is for when you'd rather listen than read.

WHAT YOU NEED

THE PAD is an iPadOS app, and it does not work alone. It needs SpeakEasy and Codex Desktop running on an Apple silicon Mac. That Mac holds the Parakeet model, the narration voices, and the bridge the nine lanes bind to.

Put both on the same Wi-Fi and choose your Mac in the app. Complete the connection and permission steps shown in Settings → Deck.

INTEGRATION BOUNDARY — THE CODEX BRIDGE USES PRIVATE, VERSION-CHECKED CODEX DESKTOP IPC AND FAILS CLOSED ON INCOMPATIBILITY. IT IS NOT PRESENTED AS A SUPPORTED PUBLIC CODEX API. SPEECH IS TRANSCRIBED BY THE PAIRED MAC; NO ARACH SPEECH SERVER SITS IN THE PATH.
