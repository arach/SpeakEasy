# Visual notes from SpeakEasy

These are simply the parts of the native SpeakEasy iPad and iPhone work that
felt especially successful to me. This is not a shared design system or a
direction for the other products.

## What worked for me

- **Fine typography.** Small labels remain sharp and deliberate. Monospaced,
  tracked type is used for metadata and changing values without taking over the
  whole interface.
- **Crisp lines.** Hairlines and control outlines feel geometric and stable at
  native scale. They do not dissolve into blur, glow, or soft shadows.
- **Complete inactive states.** Unselected and disabled controls become quieter
  without losing their border, label, or physical presence.
- **Restrained color.** A small number of colors do real work: structure,
  selection, live state, warning, and error. The palette can change completely
  from product to product.
- **Tonal surfaces.** Adjacent planes are separated first by subtle color steps
  and rules. Shadow is reserved for something that actually floats.
- **Quiet precision.** Spacing, radii, numeral alignment, and line weight feel
  consistent enough that the interface can be dense without feeling fuzzy.

Those details are the reusable part—not the flight-deck terminology, keypad
layout, product metaphors, navigation, or feature hierarchy.
